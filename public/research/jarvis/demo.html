JARVIS Index

Cultural footprints of the wish for a competent, loyal, always-on assistant: what people name their GitHub projects, what those projects do and what people read on Wikipedia. Traces of attention and building, not direct measures of desire.

Who gets the name? Repositories per 100,000 created that year

Counts of JARVIS repositories rose sharply after ChatGPT, but repositories matching "assistant" of any kind rose faster, so JARVIS's share of them fell. There is no sign of a JARVIS-specific acceleration.

What people build under the JARVIS name

Clusters of repository descriptions (MiniLM embeddings, k-means, k=8 by silhouette). The silhouette is low (0.12): read these as regions of a continuum, not distinct kinds of project. Click a cluster to see descriptions near its centre.

Fandom share of repositories that state a purpose

Fandom (explicit Iron Man, Stark or Marvel references) as a share of repositories that state a purpose; the rest is effectance (the assistant does things). The 400 reference labels were coded by an AI assistant (Claude) from a written codebook, with no independent human check yet. Corpus estimates are adjusted for each method's confusion matrix (adjusted classify-and-count); brackets are 95% intervals. The two periods cannot be told apart.
